The name

A buffalo jump is a place where nothing was wasted.

For thousands of years, Plains nations harvested bison by guiding herds over a cliff edge — a pishkun. It took the whole community to do it, and the whole animal was used afterward: meat, hide, bone, sinew, horn.

That's the standard we cook to. Ingredients that belong to this continent. Techniques that were here first. Nothing thrown away, nothing borrowed without knowing whose it is.

We are the only full-service, year-round Native American food service in New York City — and we would like some company.

Our story

What happens when a Lakota cook meets a Taino New Yorker.

Buffalo Jump NYC started with a visit — a Sicangu Lakota cook from the Rosebud Sioux Reservation coming to New York, meeting a Borikua Taino New Yorker, and deciding the city's food scene was overdue for re-indigenizing.

“Re-Claim and Re-Indigenous food culture in NYC — and hopefully someday the country and the world.” — Buffalo Jump NYC

Co-owner

The working menu

Bison, deer, wild rice, frybread, wojapi.

The menu moves with the season and with wherever we're parked that week. Here's what comes around most.

Frybread, four ways

Savory to sweet

  • Bison Chili

    Slow-cooked bison chili over hot frybread. The one people come back for.

  • Wojapi

    Traditional berry sauce — dark, thick, barely sweetened — spooned over the top.

  • Honey

    Golden honey and nothing else. The way it's been eaten forever.

  • Strawberry Maple

    Strawberry maple syrup, poured heavy. Bring napkins.
